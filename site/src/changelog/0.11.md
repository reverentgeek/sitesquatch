Thinking about moving a site from Jekyll, Hugo, MkDocs or Eleventy? This release is about making that move feel less like hauling a canoe over a mountain. We moved real sites with coding agents, wrote down every place they stumbled, and turned each stumble into something ssqch does for you.

## The headlines

- **Scout before you hike.** `ssqch scout` reads your old site and tells you what the move takes, with file and line for each thing to do. Run it again halfway, and it shows what's left.
- **Proof the move is done.** `ssqch baseline` builds your site with its old generator in fresh copies, and `ssqch diff` groups the same change across files, so one fix can clear hundreds of pages at once.
- **Bring your old templates along.** `jekyllTemplates` and `mkdocsTemplates` give a theme the `page`, `site`, `config` and `nav` it expects. Material for MkDocs' own templates now run as written, where the httpx move needed about 220 lines of its own to fake that.
- **Your Markdown, read as written.** MkDocs' admonitions and tabs, Hugo's shortcodes, links to `.md` files, and Python-Markdown's habits all have options now.
- **Filters that know where they are.** A filter can read `this.page`, and `safe()` lets it hand back HTML.

## What you might need to change

- **`ssqch import` is now `ssqch scout`.** It changes nothing unless you ask: `--write-draft` writes the new files, and `--patch` writes edits for you to review and apply.
- **Jinja templates read as Jinja reads them.** Each `.jinja` template drops its last newline, so an include no longer adds a blank line (add an empty line at the end of a template to keep one), and `tojson` sorts a dict's keys.
